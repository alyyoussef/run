01

The brief

Design a guided running session for a consumer fitness product. The watch helps during the run. The phone is for before and after.

The session

A person starts a 5km guided run. The product should stay useful while they are moving.

What to show

  • Pace
  • Heart rate
  • Cadence
  • Elapsed time
  • Distance

When to speak up

If they fall behind target pace, or if heart rate goes past a safe range.

02

Before drawing screens

I read the assignment, then wrote down the things I needed to decide first. None of this showed up as a screen. It shaped every screen after.

  1. How people run

    Two legs. One leg and a prosthetic. One leg and running sticks. The watch still has to be usable in each case — large enough targets, few taps, nothing that needs two hands.

  2. Units

    Pace, heart rate, cadence, time, and distance can be shown in different units. That choice belongs in setup, on the phone, before the run starts.

  3. What “off track” means

    Falling behind a target pace, and a safe heart rate range with a little give on either side. Those limits should be the person’s, not a hidden default.

03

The smallest face first

I looked at their watch sizes and designed for the smallest one: a 1.39" round display. If a layout holds there, it has room to grow on larger watches.

Type is Inter, never smaller than 12px. The metric each person cares about most is the largest thing on the face. Everything else steps down.

04

Three people to design against

No user profiles came with the brief. I looked at who often buys a smartwatch under $200 and wrote three short, made-up portraits. They are gender-neutral on purpose. They are not research — they kept the screens from drifting toward an “average” runner.

Chris · 68

Retired HR

Recently joined a seniors’ running club. The group meets once a week, as much for the people as for the run.

Needs
Reduced vision. Large type and buttons. One action at a time.
Looks at first
Distance, then time, then heart rate.

Sam · 52

Restaurant manager

Works on a sports car as a hobby. Trying to run three times a week to help manage cholesterol.

Needs
Gets overwhelmed by busy screens. Prefers a quiet, simple layout.
Looks at first
Pace, then time, then heart rate.

Jordan · 38

Logistics coordinator

Runs to take the edge off work and keep burnout at bay.

Needs
Color blindness (red and green look similar). Wants more detail and control.
Looks at first
Pace, time, cadence, then heart rate.

05

What belongs on the watch

During a run, the wrist is bouncing. A glance has to be enough. The phone is for sitting still — before the start, and after the finish.

On the watch

Now

  • Start, pause, and end the run
  • The numbers that matter in this moment
  • A short alert if pace is behind, or heart rate is too high

On the phone

Before and after

  • Set the 5km goal, units, and pace / heart-rate ranges
  • Full recap when the run is over
  • Extra charts and history, if someone wants them

07

Choices and tradeoffs

There is no single right layout. These are the calls I made, and what I gave up to make them.

Type and size

Inter, 12px minimum. The lead metric is larger than the rest. Chris goes further: one metric per screen.

Alerts

Only two interruptions while running: behind pace, or heart rate past the person’s safe range. Everything else can wait.

Color

Jordan cannot rely on red and green. Status is also shown with size, position, and simple shapes.

What I traded

Designing for the smallest round face meant less on the watch and more on the phone. Three versions took more time than one generic runner, but the screens stayed honest to different needs.
