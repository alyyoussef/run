In a working environment

I would start conversations with:

Project lead

I would talk with the project manager or lead to understand the goals, so I can deliver.

Technical team

I would ask what the devices can do: features, limits, and advantages, so the design can make the most of the hardware.

Marketing and sales

I would ask what target users value, what they expect, who the competitors are, and how each product is positioned.

Design team

I would learn how they approach the design system, interactions, color, and typography, and most importantly, any user personas that already shape the work.

02

Research

I read the brief several times so I understood what was expected, and what to deliver. Then I looked through Vitalist.co and followed a link to Reebok smartwatches. On connect.reebok.com/smartwatches, the Reebok Rush stood out: it has the smallest screen, at 1.39". That fit the brief, so I designed the prototype for it.

Then I did a short desk-research pass, some of it by hand, some with AI, to understand who might buy a smartwatch under $200 for a ~5km run. From that, I built three mock user personas to inform the design. The aim was to justify decisions for different people. Users may expect some personalization; personas helped me give each person a more fitting experience.

03

Data

The brief asked for these numbers during the session:

  • Pace
  • Heart rate
  • Cadence
  • Elapsed time
  • Distance

During a run, people deal with heat, cold, humidity, rain, or snow. Effort, terrain, and gear can make a glance harder. The wrist is moving, so small text is easy to miss. Age, fitness level, and accessibility needs add to that.

04

Interactions

Horizontal swipe is a familiar smartwatch interaction that I used in this prototype. Please note, for simplicity, I implemented a swipe-left gesture to navigate the Figma prototypes.

05

Personalization

Personalization is increasingly expected in digital products. I developed three personas representing different physical abilities and accessibility needs to ensure the experience felt tailored and inclusive, rather than one-size-fits-all.

Chris Sam Jordan

Chris · 68

Retired HR worker

Recently joined a seniors’ running club as a social activity. The group meets once a week.

Accessibility
Reduced vision. Small digital text is hard to read.
Personalized experience
Large text and numbers. One metric per screen.
Metrics, in order
Distance, run time, heart rate.
Open Chris’s prototype in Figma

Sam · 52

Chain restaurant manager

Would rather watch motorsport on TV, but is trying to run three times a week to help manage cholesterol, as their doctor recommended.

Accessibility
Easily overwhelmed by visual complexity. Prefers a simple interface.
Personalized experience
A simple, clean look. Racing flags for motivation, based on their interest in motorsport.
Metrics, in order
Pace, run time, heart rate.
Open Sam’s prototype in Figma

Jordan · 38

Logistics coordinator

Casually runs to help relieve work-related stress and prevent burnout.

Accessibility
Color blindness. Difficulty telling red from green.
Personalized experience
Detail-oriented. Wants to see all the metrics at once. Colors fitting their accessibility needs.
Metrics, in order
Pace, run time, distance, heart rate, cadence.
